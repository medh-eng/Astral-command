# VYUHA — seven campaign chapters

Task 4 · Phase C writing draft, not shipped campaign content. Sanjaya's words below are newly written dialogue, never scripture quotations. Lessons and victory conditions are game designs. References identify formation descriptions, not historical diagrams; turning rings, hexagonal petals, rank bonuses and this chapter order are VYUHA adaptations. The lotus and needle have distinct uses within a layered array in the cited passage; they are not treated here as interchangeable names for Abhimanyu's wheel. Sarvam/native-language and Commander review remain due.

## Chakravyuha · चक्रव्यूह

**Sanjaya:** Maharaj, Abhimanyu knew how to enter the circle, but leaving it was another matter. Courage carried him forward; the help promised behind him could not follow. Remember that cost as you study this smaller field. The nearest opening is not always the safest road. A piece sent inward needs companions, information, and somewhere to stand when the ring turns. Do not rush to the centre because it looks important. Watch the spokes. Ask which enemy has moved, which has waited, and what your next order will be. Today, let your courage travel with a plan.

**Lesson:** Before entering the inner ring, prepare a supported route for your next move.

**Victory:** You kept your way open while the wheel closed around your enemy.

**Basis:** Mahabharata, Ganguli translation, Drona Parva, [Section XXXIV](https://original.sacred-texts.com/hin/m07/m07033.htm): Abhimanyu explains his knowledge of entry and uncertainty about escape.

## Padmavyuha · पद्मव्यूह

**Sanjaya:** Maharaj, Drona once set a lotus behind another great array, with a needle within it. Defence could have layers, each guarding what lay beyond. Here our lotus is smaller, its petals drawn upon stone for your lesson. A guarded warrior does not stand alone. Two companions can make a modest rank difficult to dislodge, but moving one away may loosen the whole shape. Look beyond the piece you wish to save. Consider what protects its protectors. Let an impatient enemy meet your outer guard while you keep a useful path between the petals and the fort.

**Lesson:** Maintain mutual support instead of defending each piece in isolation.

**Victory:** Your patient guard held firm, and every petal sheltered the whole army.

**Basis:** Mahabharata, Ganguli translation, Drona Parva, [Section LXXXVII](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/m07/m07084.htm), describes a lotus array behind the larger formation. The hexagonal board is a game adaptation.

## Kraunchavyuha · क्रौञ्चव्यूह

**Sanjaya:** Maharaj, the epic describes an army arranged like a crane, with warriors appointed to its different parts. A pointed front does not make the rest of the army unimportant. The wings must answer when the enemy presses around the beak. On this field, you will feel the temptation to send your strongest piece straight ahead. First look to either side. A narrow advance can gain ground and still leave its leader stranded. Bring a second threat into reach. Keep a useful reserve. If the front must turn, give it room to turn without abandoning everyone behind it.

**Lesson:** Support a central advance with threats on its flanks.

**Victory:** One sharp advance succeeded because both wings stayed ready to answer danger.

**Basis:** Mahabharata, Ganguli translation, Bhishma Parva, [Section LI](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/m06/m06051.htm), names the Krauncha array; adjoining battle descriptions supply its crane imagery. Facing mechanics require separate implementation.

## Makaravyuha · मकरव्यूह

**Sanjaya:** Maharaj, the Makara gives its name to an array in the epic, a great creature of the waters given shape by an army. Our lesson borrows its closing jaws. One warrior draws attention while another makes the next square dangerous. Neither needs to be the strongest on the field. What matters is that they arrive together. If one attacks too early, the enemy may take it before the second can help. Keep your pieces near enough to support the strike, yet leave them a way to move. A pincer succeeds through timing, not merely through its shape.

**Lesson:** Create a supported Makara strike before committing either side of the pincer.

**Victory:** You closed the jaws together and left no warrior fighting there alone.

**Basis:** Mahabharata, Ganguli translation, Bhishma Parva, [Section LXXV](https://original.sacred-texts.com/hin/m06/m06075.htm), describes the Pandava Makara array. VYUHA's +1 pincer is a game rule, not a claimed ancient rule.

## Suchivyuha · सूचीव्यूह

**Sanjaya:** Maharaj, within Drona's lotus stood a dense array called the needle. Its name asks you to notice concentration: many duties arranged around a narrow purpose. On our field, choose one lane with care. A chariot may see a long road, but a single friendly piece can close it. An engineer may need to lead where a greater warrior cannot clear the trap. Move each companion with the next in mind. Do not empty every other part of the board to feed your advance. Keep enough watch behind you that an opening for your army does not become an opening for theirs.

**Lesson:** Clear and preserve a useful lane without exposing your own fort.

**Victory:** Your chosen lane stayed open because each step prepared the next advance.

**Basis:** Mahabharata, Ganguli translation, Drona Parva, [Section LXXXVII](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/m07/m07084.htm), explicitly places a needle array within the lotus. Lane puzzles are a game adaptation.

## Shyenavyuha · श्येनव्यूह

**Sanjaya:** Maharaj, the Syena array of the epic takes the shape of a hawk. Think of the bird's attention before its descent: it does not spend every moment striking. Here you must watch an enemy whose ranks you cannot yet read. A short advance, a reveal, or a quiet change of position can show where the guard is thin. The first tempting target may be bait. Keep your swift piece ready until the information justifies the journey. When you finally move, know both the square you seek and the answer you expect. Patience is useful when it prepares a decision.

**Lesson:** Use information and positioning to prepare a decisive cavalry or chariot strike.

**Victory:** You waited above the noise, then struck where their guard grew thin.

**Basis:** Mahabharata, Ganguli translation, Bhishma Parva, [Section LXIX](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/m06/m06069.htm), names the Syena array and explains its hawk shape in a translator's note. The lesson is original interpretation.

## Sarvatobhadra · सर्वतोभद्र

**Sanjaya:** Maharaj, Bhishma arranged warriors at the front, wings, middle, and rear of the Sarvatobhadra. Attention belonged to the whole army. You have now learned to enter, guard, support, concentrate, and wait. This field asks you to remember all of them together. While your attack grows stronger on one side, look at the other. While the hour favours you, ask what will happen when it changes. A fort with its gate standing still needs protection. A quiet enemy is still making choices. Keep a reserve, read the field again, and let no part of your army disappear from your thinking.

**Lesson:** Balance attack with defence across every approach to your fort.

**Victory:** Every side stood ready, and your whole army carried this victory home.

**Basis:** Mahabharata, Ganguli translation, Bhishma Parva, [Section C](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/m06/m06100.htm), describes the appointments; the square facing all directions is the translator's interpretation. The board is a VYUHA adaptation.
